# Gen Z Women (18–24, US) and Self-Improvement / Wellness Apps — Research Brief

Date: 2026-10-12. Sources: industry reports, app stores, SEC filings, company statements, peer-reviewed studies, major press. Numbers quoted as published; third-party estimates flagged; gaps stated. Citations [n] are listed under Sources.

## 1. Spending and subscriptions

**No public dataset isolates US Gen Z women's app-subscription spend.** Sensor Tower, Appfigures and data.ai publish category totals but no public gender split for Health & Fitness spend. What exists:

- Category size: Health & Fitness apps generated **$4.5B in IAP revenue in 2025 (+13% YoY)** on 3.96B downloads (+0.8%); growth is revenue-led, not install-led [1]. Jan 2025 alone: $385M (+10%) [2]. The US captured more than half of global "Medical Tracking" (incl. women's health) spend in 2024 [2].
- Gen Z subscription behaviour (Bango, 5,000 US subscribers, Sep 2024): 25% of Gen Z pay for 6–10 subscriptions; Gen Z is less likely than millennials to spend >$100/month; it is the generation most likely to pay for an app it isn't using; 57% would accept a higher phone bill if subscriptions were bundled [3].
- NMI (1,000 US adults, May 2025): subscription model preferred by 69% of millennials and 66% of Gen Z [4].
- Who pays: only 46% of 18–24s fully pay for their own subscriptions vs 80% of 35+ (Savanta/Minna) — cost-sharing and parent-paid plans are common [5].
- Wellness as protected spend (McKinsey Future of Wellness 2025, 9,000+ consumers US/UK/DE/CN): ~30% of Gen Z/millennials increased wellness budgets "significantly" in the past year vs 23% of older adults; they would cut fashion/leisure before wellness; top priorities sleep, health, appearance; Gen Z reports unmet needs in mental health [6][7].
- Aflac (via eMarketer): 82% of Gen Z spend on self-care (incl. fitness apps, mental health care) vs 52% across all generations [8].
- Gender gap in wellness spend (BeautyMatter, US): women $124/month vs men $94 on beauty, fitness and wellness [9].
- Gender proxies: paid Calm subscribers 79.9% female (n=10,981) [10]; meditation-app users 68.3% female (JMIR 2026) [11]; Pinterest ad audience 70.3% female [12]; 25% of US women 18–25 had downloaded Co-Star by 2021 [13].
- Rock Health 2024: women track more health metrics than men (3.11 vs 2.85) but fewer digitally, and are **less likely to trust apps/social media health info** [14].
- Gen Z vs millennials willingness to pay: no clean head-to-head. Gen Z holds more subscriptions at lower per-sub value, relies more on shared/parent-paid plans, and tolerates unused subscriptions most [3][5].

## 4. Psychology: why Gen Z women engage

**Baseline mental-health data (US unless noted)**
- Gallup/Walton (May 2025, n=3,793, ages 13–28): 45% thriving (3-year low); Gen Z adults 39%; **adult Gen Z women 37%, down from 46% in 2024** [70]. Gallup 2024: ~half often feel anxious, ~1 in 5 often depressed [71].
- Deloitte 2025 (23,000+ in 44 countries): 40% of Gen Z stressed/anxious all or most of the time; only 52% rate mental health good [72].
- APA Stress in America 2025: 22% of 18–34s rate stress 8–10/10; women 22% vs men 18%; 65% of 18–34s report AI-related stress [73].
- Pew (Apr 2025): teen girls 25% vs boys 14% say social media hurts their mental health; 50% of girls say it hurts sleep [74].
- Harmony Healthcare IT (May 2025, n=1,010): 42% of Gen Z report a diagnosed mental-health condition; anxiety most common [75].
- Ipsos (31 countries, 2023): Gen Z women are the demographic most likely to report extreme stress and depression [76].

**Mechanisms with evidence**
- Perfectionism is rising: socially prescribed perfectionism up ~32% across college cohorts 1989–2016 and accelerating since the early 2000s (Curran & Hill, Psychological Bulletin 2019/2026) [77]. Gallup/Damour 2024: adolescents feeling pressure to be perfect are 2–3x more likely to report negative emotions [78].
- Upward social comparison: preregistered experiment (Weber et al., Social Sciences, Jul 2025) found watching "That Girl" videos **lowered mood and body satisfaction and raised dieting intentions**, with effects concentrated in women who compared upward [48]. Qualitative "glow-up imperative" study (2025): guilt/shame when time, money and energy demands aren't met [64]. Cybersmile 2023: 86% of young people feel pressured to be perfect by social content [79].
- Control and identity: creators describe routine videos as regaining control amid job uncertainty [58]; astrology apps function as "a version of therapy" and identity scaffolding [13]; Penn research: self-optimisation content "doesn't seem to result in increased happiness" [80].
- Community and accountability: two-thirds of Gen Z Strava users made friends through fitness groups; Gen Z 39% more likely than Gen X to use fitness to meet people [37][38]; Finch grows through Reddit/Discord communities [18].
- Soft vs hard discipline: both convert. Duolingo's guilt-based streaks scale to 52.7M DAU [34]; Finch's no-punishment design is its stated differentiator [21]. The pendulum swings: the 2025 "Great Lock-In" is framed as Gen Z rejecting millennials' "soft life" [57]; Opal found mental-health framing didn't convert Gen Z but productivity did [30].
- Burnout as spend driver: McKinsey ties younger consumers' wellness prioritisation to self-reported burnout [7]; Gen Z workers weigh psychological safety and balance more heavily [81].
- Caveat: a 2025 longitudinal study found distress drives TikTok use more than the reverse — content is both cause and coping tool [82].

## 5. Failures and backlash

- **"That girl" burnout critique**: therapists call the ideal unattainable and privilege-based; Her Campus and others label it toxic productivity; the 2025 experiment supplies the first causal evidence of harm [48][49][83].
- **75 Hard**: expert warnings about intensity; softened versions (75 Soft) gained traction instead [84].
- **Winter Arc**: criticised for reviving diet culture and consumerism (gym kit, supplements); New Statesman (Oct 2025) on its "narcissistic cult"; a goal-setting expert called it "the wrong way to reach goals" [85][86][87].
- **Streak shame (Duolingo)**: "you made Duo sad" notifications documented as guilt/loss-aversion tactics [88]; Gen Z turned the owl into a meme enemy [89]; the Apr 2025 "AI-first" memo triggered deliberate streak-breaking protests; growth continued but the CEO conceded low-end guidance and the stock fell sharply from its May 2025 peak [90].
- **Paywall backlash**: 18,464 Google Play reviews across six habit trackers — paywall/subscription is the #1 complaint (450 reviews, 1,197 helpful votes); anger comes from limits appearing *after* days of invested data and from undisclosed trials, not price itself (author is a competitor) [91]. Fabulous: billing/auto-renewal complaints [92]. Finch: Android vs iOS price gap, surprise upsells [93]. Me+: "unexpected charges" [27]. Structured: free tier "feels like a teaser" [94].
- **Mental-health app fatigue**: Appfigures (May 2024): Calm and Headspace downloads down 61% and 74% since 2018 [46].
- **BeReal**: 73.5M users (Aug 2022) to ~23M (early 2024); downloads down 60% in 2024; the daily notification "became a burden" and brands diluted authenticity [95].
- **Privacy rejection (Flo)**: FTC settlement 2021; post-Roe deletions and app-switching in 2022; Aug 2025 California jury found Meta violated privacy law using Flo data (Flo settled before verdict) [24][96].
- **Opal**: Gen Z mental-health positioning failed in testing [30].
- **Morning shed**: dermatologists and L'Officiel call it a step backwards for self-love [60][97].
- **Pilates princess fatigue**: body-image critique (CNN) and reported shift toward "sporty" over flawless [63][98].
- **Hustle refusal**: lazy girl job, soft life, bed rotting signal rejection of grind culture, yet each drew its own "defeatist"/"privileged" critique [69][65].

## Key takeaways

1. No public source isolates Gen Z women's wellness-app spend; the defensible story is category revenue growth ($4.5B, +13%) plus strong female skew in meditation/cycle/astrology audiences.
2. Gen Z holds many subscriptions at low per-sub value; 54% of 18–24s don't fully pay for their own — price under $10/month, annual at $30–70, and family/shared plans matter.
3. Finch is the category benchmark: 2M daily users, 763K iOS ratings at 4.9, grown by word of mouth with gentle, non-punitive mechanics.
4. Wellness/habit apps don't crack Gen Z top-50 downloads; they win on retention and revenue, so design for day-30 return, not virality alone.
5. "Soft" framing sells to this audience (Finch, cozy cardio, 75 Soft) but "hard" framing (Great Lock-In, Duolingo streaks) also converts — the winning posture is structured flexibility: streak repair, rest days, no restart-from-zero.
6. The strongest evidence of harm is upward comparison to aesthetic routines ("that girl"): avoid aspirational-perfection creative; use "survive the day" realism.
7. Perfectionism is rising and adult Gen Z women's thriving fell to 37% in 2025 — shame-based engagement loops are a churn risk, not a growth lever.
8. The #1 review complaint is paywalls that appear after investment or trials that aren't disclosed; transparent trial messaging is a retention feature.
9. Trust is the gate: women trust app health info less than men, and Flo's privacy saga shows data handling is a brand issue for this segment.
10. Community/accountability (Strava clubs, hot-girl-walk meetups, Finch Discord) is the most durable engagement mechanic documented across sources.
